## Building an AI Team Effectiveness Simulator

### Why simulation beats reading

This project builds an AI-driven simulator to operationalize Lencioni’s Five Dysfunctions model through controlled interaction with resistant personas.

Instead of passively reading theory, the system forces real-time diagnosis and intervention across all five layers of the dysfunction pyramid. Each interaction is mapped to observable behavior and then tied to measurable business outcomes. The simulator creates a closed loop where leadership actions can be tested, evaluated, and refined under pressure conditions that resemble real team dynamics.

### Setting up the environment

The environment establishes the simulation and visualization layers.

Mermaid Live Editor is used to construct the dysfunction pyramid and KPI scorecard, providing a visual baseline of team health. A structured simulation file defines personas, prompts, and expected behaviors. Claude Desktop acts as the execution layer, running the simulation and validating that each persona responds consistently with its defined behavioral pattern.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_fe0icxjy)

### Meet the Forge Robotics team

The system models a cross-functional engineering team with distinct behavioral patterns.

Each persona represents a different dysfunction signal. Maya reflects trust breakdown through silence, Sam represents conflict avoidance through over-agreement, Dev demonstrates accountability gaps through deflection, and Jordan focuses on individual metrics over collective results. These personas create a controlled environment for testing leadership interventions.

## Diagnosing Team Dysfunction: The Before KPI Scorecard

### Diagnostic approach

The system begins with a baseline diagnosis across all five dysfunction layers.

Each persona is mapped to a dysfunction based on observed behavior. The scorecard quantifies these dysfunctions, creating a measurable starting point. This establishes a reference for evaluating whether interventions produce meaningful improvement.

### Lowest-scoring dysfunction layer

The weakest layer is identified as absence of trust.

Maya’s minimal communication and visible disengagement signal a lack of psychological safety. This creates downstream effects across all other layers, preventing conflict, reducing accountability, and ultimately impacting results.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_2zk1nt9r)

## Building Vulnerability-Based Trust with Maya

### Breaking through the silent-skeptic wall

The intervention focuses on initiating vulnerability as a leadership action.

A deliberate trust opener introduces personal accountability and removes pressure from the response. This creates space for Maya to engage without defensive behavior. The approach is designed to shift interaction from guarded responses to open communication.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_r8nw7jot)

### Vulnerability-based vs. predictive trust

The system distinguishes between two types of trust.

Predictive trust develops over time through consistent behavior, while vulnerability-based trust can be established quickly through deliberate exposure. The simulation reinforces that higher layers of the pyramid cannot function without establishing vulnerability-based trust first.

## Mining Productive Conflict with Sam

### Converting harmony-keeping into honest debate

The intervention targets artificial harmony by introducing structured tension.

The approach acknowledges Sam’s behavior while challenging the absence of disagreement. This reframes conflict as a necessary input for better decisions rather than a disruption to team cohesion.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_60oqxrj5)

### Artificial harmony vs. productive ideological conflict

The system differentiates between surface agreement and meaningful debate.

Artificial harmony creates alignment without conviction, leading to weak execution. Productive conflict enables stronger decisions by surfacing competing ideas. The simulation demonstrates that avoiding conflict introduces more risk than engaging in it.

## Driving Commitment Through Clarity and Buy-In

### Getting the team to commit without consensus

The system enforces commitment through structured decision-making.

A decision memo defines ownership, timelines, and rationale. This removes ambiguity and ensures that all team members understand the decision, regardless of initial agreement. Commitment is measured through execution, not consensus.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_yqtayjra)

### Why hearing dissent is required for real commitment

Commitment depends on participation, not agreement.

Allowing dissent ensures that all perspectives are considered before finalizing decisions. Without this step, resistance shifts from open disagreement to passive non-compliance. The simulation shows that commitment fails when dissent is suppressed.

## Delivering Peer Accountability Without Escalation

### Holding Dev accountable peer-to-peer

The intervention removes authority as the primary enforcement mechanism.

Accountability is framed around observable behavior and prior commitments rather than hierarchy. This forces alignment around shared expectations instead of positional power.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_ainqj8di)

### What made the call-out land

The effectiveness comes from specificity and neutrality.

The feedback references measurable gaps and ties directly to prior commitments. This eliminates ambiguity and prevents deflection, making the conversation outcome-focused rather than personal.

## Focusing the Team on Collective Results

### Designing a scoreboard that defeats individual status-seeking

The system shifts incentives from individual output to team performance.

A shared scoreboard defines success at the team level, aligning all contributions toward a single measurable outcome. This removes competing priorities and reinforces collaboration.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_nlplz908)

### Making individual metrics a component of team success

Individual performance is redefined as a contributor to team outcomes.

Metrics are structured so that personal achievements only matter when they improve the collective result. This eliminates isolated optimization and reinforces dependency across roles.

## Capstone Meeting: All Five Layers Simultaneously

### Running the integrated capstone challenge

The capstone validates the system under full complexity.

All five dysfunction layers are addressed in a single interaction, requiring simultaneous application of trust-building, conflict management, accountability, and results alignment. The outcome is measured through a post-intervention KPI scorecard.

### Hardest layer to hold under pressure

Commitment is the most difficult layer to sustain.

Unlike other layers, it requires maintaining focus through multiple competing signals. Failure to close with clear commitments results in loss of progress across all other layers.

### Tracing the causal chain from action to business outcome

Each intervention is linked directly to measurable impact.

A trust-based action leads to surfaced risks, which prevents downstream failures. This establishes a clear connection between leadership behavior and delivery outcomes.

## Before vs. After: Proving the KPI Movement

### After Action Review and transfer planning

The system validates effectiveness through measurable improvement.

The before and after scorecards quantify changes across all dysfunction layers. The analysis identifies which interventions produced the largest impact and why they were effective.

### Personal dysfunction commitment

The system extends beyond simulation into real-world application.

A personal dysfunction is identified and translated into observable behavior. This ensures that learning is not theoretical but applied through measurable actions.

![Image](https://learn.nextwork.org/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/d48bf50d-a7ea-4fee-8c4b-8c3ea2163abf_oksg4wuu)

### Biggest delta and what caused it

The largest improvements occur in trust, accountability, and results.

Trust improves through vulnerability-based leadership actions, accountability improves through structured peer feedback, and results improve through aligned incentives. These changes compound over time, reinforcing system stability.
