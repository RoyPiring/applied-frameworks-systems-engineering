## Project Overview

This project turns the FBI hostage-negotiation tactics from Chris Voss's Never Split the Difference into a repeatable, AI-coached rehearsal system for a real salary renegotiation. Across structured roleplay rounds it drills all nine tactics, scores each one before and after, and captures reusable script cards so the techniques hold under live pressure.

### Goals and preparation

In this project, I prepared for an upcoming salary renegotiation by practicing FBI negotiation techniques through AI-powered roleplay. My objective was to build a structured approach that would help me communicate confidently while keeping the conversation collaborative instead of confrontational. Rather than relying on instinct during a high-pressure discussion, I wanted a repeatable framework that would allow me to stay composed regardless of how the conversation evolved.

Throughout the training, I focused on developing a calm delivery using the "late-night FM DJ" voice, sharpening my use of Mirroring and Labeling techniques, and learning how to respond to difficult situations with Calibrated Questions instead of immediate counter-offers. By repeatedly practicing realistic scenarios, I became more comfortable managing tension and maintaining control of the conversation while encouraging the other person to work toward a solution with me.

## Setting Up the Negotiation Coach

### Configuring the environment

I began by setting up the negotiation environment and preparing the supporting tools required for the exercises. This included running the project setup script, generating the required project files, installing the voice components, and configuring the Cowork Project with the AI Coach system prompt. After completing the environment configuration, I described my real-world negotiation scenario and completed a self-assessment across all nine FBI negotiation tactics to establish a measurable starting point.

### Baseline self-assessment

My initial assessment resulted in a score of 12 out of 27, showing that I was generally stronger with defensive communication than proactive negotiation techniques. While I understood each tactic conceptually, several had never been used during a live negotiation.

The assessment highlighted Tactical Empathy, Accusation Audits, "That's Right," the No Gambit, Ackerman bargaining, and Black Swans as my weakest areas. Identifying these gaps early gave me a clear direction for the remaining exercises and established a baseline that I could compare against after completing the training.

## Mastering the Mirror Technique Under Pressure

### Practicing mirroring drills

I completed three rounds of mirror practice, with each round increasing the difficulty and emotional pressure of the conversation. The progression allowed me to experience how easily conversations can accelerate under stress and reinforced the importance of slowing the pace instead of trying to fill every silence. As the scenarios became more challenging, I concentrated on repeating key emotional language naturally and allowing silence to encourage the other person to continue speaking.

After each exercise, I reviewed the AI Coach's feedback and refined my responses before moving to the next round. Once the practice was complete, I saved my final Mirror Script Card for use during future negotiations.

### Scores and weakest criterion

I achieved a perfect score of 18 out of 18, earning full marks across all three rounds. Although every category received the highest score, Precision remained the area requiring the most discipline because it is the first skill to deteriorate when conversations become stressful. The exercises reinforced that effective mirroring depends on saying less, choosing the right words, and allowing silence to do the work instead of expanding on the other person's statements.

## Labeling Emotions to Earn 'That's Right'

### Emotional labeling practice

The next phase focused on identifying emotions instead of reacting to positions. I began by creating an Accusation Audit that anticipated the concerns and assumptions my manager could bring into the negotiation before the conversation even started. From there, I completed three rounds of emotional labeling exercises, practicing how to recognize and verbalize emotions without assigning blame or attempting to persuade too early.

These sessions demonstrated how thoughtful labels encourage the other person to feel understood, creating opportunities for more productive discussions. After completing the drills, I compiled both the Accusation Audit and my strongest labeling sequence into a reusable Label Script Card.

### Surface-level vs identity-level labels

One of the most valuable lessons from this exercise was understanding the difference between describing circumstances and recognizing identity. Surface-level labels acknowledge obvious emotions or situations, but identity-level labels recognize how those situations affect someone's values, leadership, or personal standards.

As the conversations progressed, I noticed that stronger labels shifted the discussion away from defending positions and toward collaborative problem solving. Reaching the point where the counterpart responded with "That's right" demonstrated genuine understanding rather than simple agreement and represented one of the most important milestones in the negotiation process.

## Using Calibrated Questions to Drive the Negotiation

### Making the counterpart solve the problem

During this exercise, I practiced using Calibrated Questions under increasingly difficult scenarios while intentionally avoiding salary numbers, counter-offers, and "Why" questions. This forced me to remain curious instead of argumentative and encouraged the conversation to move toward solutions rather than disagreements.

Each round reinforced the value of allowing the other person to participate in solving the problem. Instead of attempting to convince the counterpart that my position was reasonable, I learned to guide the discussion with thoughtful questions that encouraged collaboration and revealed additional options.

![Image](https://nextwork.ai/refreshed_maroon_timid_jujube/uploads/c9c60d20-6f31-4fce-9f93-0f264a4234fe_ofielf42)

### Resisting counter-offers

The simulations clearly demonstrated that resisting the urge to make an immediate counter-offer changed the direction of the conversation. Questions such as "How am I supposed to..." and "What would it take..." encouraged the counterpart to move beyond base salary discussions and begin exploring other possibilities, including bonuses, title changes, equity, and off-cycle compensation adjustments.

Rather than negotiating against myself, I allowed the conversation to uncover alternatives that may not have surfaced through traditional bargaining. This exercise showed how carefully chosen questions can shift responsibility for finding solutions to the other party while maintaining a collaborative tone.

## Closing with No, Ackerman, and Black Swan Tactics

### Combining advanced closing techniques

The final capstone combined every negotiation technique into a complete end-to-end simulation. Throughout four progressively challenging scenarios, I practiced using strategic "No" questions, conducting an Accusation Audit, following the Ackerman bargaining sequence, and identifying potential Black Swans that could significantly change the negotiation.

Bringing every technique together demonstrated how each tactic complements the others throughout an entire conversation instead of functioning independently. By the final round, I was able to transition naturally between empathy, questioning, bargaining, and information gathering without relying on scripted responses.

### Pre-score to post-score gains

After completing the capstone simulation, my final assessment increased from 12 out of 27 to a perfect 27 out of 27, representing an overall gain of fifteen points. The largest gains came from the offensive negotiation techniques that initially received the lowest scores, including Tactical Empathy, Accusation Audits, "That's Right," the No Gambit, Ackerman bargaining, and Black Swans.

The remaining tactics, including Mirroring, Labeling, and Calibrated Questions, also became more consistent under pressure. Comparing the initial and final assessments demonstrated measurable progress and confirmed that repeated practice transformed concepts I had only studied into techniques I could confidently apply during live conversations.

## Going Further: Adaptation and Teaching

### Adaptation speed and archetype shifts

One of the more challenging exercises required adapting immediately when the counterpart unexpectedly shifted from a transactional discussion to an emotional response. Instead of reassuring the other person or defending my position, I identified the underlying emotion and responded with a label that acknowledged what they were experiencing.

Successfully changing tactics within a single response reinforced the importance of remaining flexible while maintaining the overall negotiation strategy. Recognizing emotional shifts quickly helped keep the conversation productive without making unnecessary concessions.

### Teaching to reinforce learning

The final reinforcement exercise focuses on teaching the Mirror technique to another person. Although this step has not yet been completed, I selected Mirroring because it is one of the easiest techniques to demonstrate and immediately illustrates how active listening changes the flow of a conversation.

Teaching the concept will provide another opportunity to reinforce my own understanding while helping someone else experience how a simple communication technique can significantly ease difficult discussions.

## Reflections and Key Takeaways

This project combined Claude Desktop, the Cowork Project feature, a custom PowerShell setup script, Python, the edge-tts library, and the Pocket Playbook to create a practical negotiation training environment. Working through realistic roleplay scenarios allowed me to practice all nine FBI negotiation techniques in situations that closely resembled the conversations I expect to have professionally.

Beyond learning individual tactics, the project demonstrated how Mirroring, Labeling, Tactical Empathy, Accusation Audits, and Calibrated Questions work together to reduce tension, encourage collaboration, and maintain composure throughout high-pressure conversations.

### Personal learning goals

I completed this project to build a structured and repeatable negotiation process that I can confidently apply during salary discussions and other difficult workplace conversations. Repeated AI roleplay helped reduce the uncertainty associated with high-pressure negotiations while strengthening my ability to remain calm, communicate intentionally, and adapt as conversations evolve.

Looking ahead, I plan to continue developing these behavioral communication techniques and apply them beyond salary negotiations to leadership conversations, conflict resolution, mentoring, and team management, where empathy and thoughtful communication are equally important to successful outcomes.
